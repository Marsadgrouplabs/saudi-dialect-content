# Research Sources and What They Support

Research freeze: 2026-09-26

## High-priority academic / corpus sources

1. Alhaissoni, Alanoud S. A.; Yafooz, Wael M. S.; Alsaeedi, Abdullah (2026).
   "Automatic speech recognition and dialect identification for Arabic and
   Saudi dialects: a systematic literature review."
   Journal of King Saud University Computer and Information Sciences.
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44443-026-00609-6
   Supports: five major Saudi dialect families; internal diversity; code
   switching and computational challenges.

2. Alharbi, Abdullah I. et al. (2026).
   "Saudi ASWAT: A Large-Scale Corpus of Spontaneous Saudi Arabic Speech."
   LREC 2026, ACL Anthology.
   https://aclanthology.org/2026.lrec-1.124/
   Supports: nationwide five-family sampling; 55+ local varieties; real
   spontaneous speech; age/gender/regional variation.

3. Al-Rojaie, Yousef (2021).
   "Perceptual mapping of linguistic variation in Saudi Arabic dialects."
   Poznan Studies in Contemporary Linguistics 57(4):471–517.
   DOI: 10.1515/psicl-2021-0019
   Supports: five perceived major dialect areas; regional variation and social
   perception.

4. Alghamdi, Najla M. (2022).
   "Najdi and Hijazi Dialects: The Formation in Progress of a Saudi Koine."
   Journal of Research in Language & Translation, King Saud University.
   https://jrlt.ksu.edu.sa/en/node/2932
   DOI: 10.33948/JRLT-KSU-2-1-3
   Supports: emerging supra-local Saudi koine; Najdi/Hijazi influence;
   advertising data; marked local variants absent in sampled ads.

5. Alahmari, Salwa Saad (2025).
   "SADSLyC: A Corpus for Saudi Arabian Multi-dialect Identification through
   Song Lyrics." Proceedings of WACL-4, ACL Anthology.
   https://aclanthology.org/2025.wacl-1.4/
   Supports: five Saudi dialect categories in a 151,841-word / 31,358-sentence
   corpus.

6. Al-Monef, Renad; Alhuzali, Hassan; Alturayeif, Nora; Alasmari, Ashwag (2026).
   "From words to proverbs: Evaluating LLMs' linguistic and cultural competence
   in Saudi dialects with Absher." Alexandria Engineering Journal 137:25–41.
   Dataset:
   https://github.com/renad-01/Absher-Benchmark
   Supports: curated Saudi words, phrases and proverbs across Central, Western,
   Southern, Northern, Eastern and General categories; 3,094 source terms.

7. Nydell/Omar, Margaret K. (1975).
   "Saudi Arabic, Urban Hijazi Dialect: Basic Course."
   U.S. Foreign Service Institute.
   ERIC record: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED112665
   Google Books record:
   https://books.google.com/books/about/Saudi_Arabic_urban_Hijazi_Dialect.html?id=vCxjAAAAMAAJ
   Supports: urban Hijazi grammar and vocabulary (Jeddah-oriented), including
   forms such as abgha, eesh, feen, haada/haadi, etc. Historical source, so
   modern usage should be checked against current practice.

8. Binturki / Najdi syntax source:
   "Complementizer Agreement in Najdi Arabic."
   University of Kansas repository.
   https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/bitstream/handle/1808/12235/Lewis_ku_0099M_12920_DATA_1.pdf
   Supports: Najdi negation contrast involving mub and maa; discussion of
   Najdi future/want forms.

9. Almohaimeed, Noorah A. (2024).
   "Gender Differences in the Use of Negation Markers in Qassimi Arabic."
   International Journal of English Linguistics 14(4).
   https://ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/ijel/article/view/0/50454
   Supports: Qassimi mu/mub/muhub variation and supra-local leveling.

10. Almossa, Amereh Ibrahim (2024).
    "Discourse-pragmatic variation and change in Najdi Arabic."
    PhD thesis, University of York.
    https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/35587/
    Supports: Riyadh Najdi discourse variation; ma adri/madri, tayyib/tab,
    intensifiers marrah, jiddan, wallah, sidg.

11. Altamimi, Salman (2024).
    "Three Discourse Markers in the Najdi Arabic Dialect of Riyadh."
    University of Mississippi dissertation.
    https://egrove.olemiss.edu/etd/2912/
    Supports: tayyib, agool, alheen as Riyadh Najdi discourse markers.

12. Al-Zubeiry, Hameed Y. A. (2026).
    "The Discourse-Pragmatic Functions of 'Samm' in Najdi Arabic."
    Journal of Research in Language & Translation, KSU.
    https://jrlt.ksu.edu.sa/en/node/2994
    Supports: multifunctional pragmatic marker samm in Najdi interaction.

13. Albawardi, Areej (2024).
    "Detecting Qassimi Saudi Dialect in Saudi Digitally-mediated Communication:
    A Linguistic Perspective."
    International Journal of Arabic-English Studies 24(1):341–364.
    https://ijaes2011.net/index.php/IJAES/article/view/567
    Supports: spoken Qassimi phonological/morphosyntactic/semantic features can
    carry into WhatsApp writing.

14. Alrajhi, Mashael (published study page surfaced 2026).
    "The Use of Saudi Colloquial Varieties of Arabic in Saudi Formal Twitter
    Accounts." Dialectologia.
    https://revistes.ub.edu/index.php/dialectologia/article/view/52872
    Supports: in the sampled government/academic Twitter corpus, 91.67% of
    tweets were MSA and 8.33% colloquial. Useful for register selection.

15. Almoaily, Mohammad (2023).
    "Code-switching functions in online advertisements on Snapchat."
    PLOS ONE 18(7): e0287478.
    https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0287478
    Supports: Saudi influencer ads were mainly Arabic; 102 English-switch
    instances in 4,000 words, mostly availability-driven.

## Method note

No finite dictionary can truthfully contain "all Saudi terms." Saudi Arabic is
not a single codified standard and current corpus projects document dozens of
local varieties. Therefore this skill uses:
1. a high-confidence core lexicon,
2. regional profiles,
3. intensity controls,
4. anti-mixing rules,
5. a verification fallback for hyperlocal vocabulary.
