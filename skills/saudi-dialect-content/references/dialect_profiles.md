# Dialect Profiles

This file is a practical copywriting reference, not a claim that regional
boundaries are linguistically absolute.

## 1. SAUDI_NEUTRAL

Best for:
- national campaigns
- brands serving multiple Saudi cities
- first drafts when the region is unknown
- customer service at scale

Characteristics:
- modern Saudi rhythm
- high intelligibility
- low density of regional markers
- vocabulary drawn from broadly understood Saudi colloquial Arabic
- may lean mildly Najdi/Hijazi because both are influential in supra-local use

Preferred:
مو، تقدر، خلّك، طيب، خلاص، عادي، ترى، مرّة، وين، ليش

Use cautiously:
وش / إيش — choose based on tone/context rather than randomly alternating.
الحين — safe in many Saudi contexts but leans central.
أبغى / أبي — avoid if you do not need "want", or select consistently.

## 2. NAJDI_MODERN — Riyadh / central urban

High-signal anchors:
وش، أبي، تبي، نبي، الحين، موب/مو، ودي، ترى، صدق

Natural copy examples:
- وش هدفك هالفترة؟
- إذا تبي تبدأ، الحين وقتك.
- ودّك بتجربة أسهل؟
- مو لازم تغيّر كل شيء من أول يوم.

Avoid in mainstream copy:
- very local Qassimi affricated spellings
- heavy heritage vocabulary
- treating every central speaker as Qassimi

## 3. QASSIMI_LOCAL

Use only for explicit Qassim targeting, dialogue, or cultural content.

Attested local items/features in collected sources include:
مالك لوا، احتس، أولاه, and locally marked phonological/morphological features.

Modern younger Qassimi speech also shows leveling toward supra-local forms, so
do not assume the most traditional form is the most natural for a young brand.

## 4. HIJAZI_URBAN — Jeddah/Makkah/Madinah

High-signal anchors:
إيش، أبغى، تبغى، نبغى، فين، دحين، مو، مرّة

Secondary/local:
سيب، أهرج/هرجة، قوام, بزورة

Natural copy examples:
- إيش خطتك للويكند؟
- تبغى تبدأ بخطوة بسيطة؟
- دحين تقدر تحجز من الجوال.
- مرّة أسهل، ومن غير تعقيد.

Do not overload old Jeddah/Makkah neighborhood vocabulary in a modern national
brand unless the creative concept is heritage/local identity.

## 5. EASTERN_GENERAL

Dammam/Khobar audiences are highly mobile and broad. Default to Saudi-neutral
with only light eastern/Gulf flavor.

Do not equate Eastern with Qatif or Al-Ahsa.

## 6. QATIF_LOCAL

Local sources show:
ويش، حق ويش، شحوالكم/وشحالكم, plus locally distinctive lexicon and phonology.

Use چ/گ only in explicit local/dialogue mode. Standard brand orthography should
normally avoid spelling every pronunciation phonetically.

## 7. AHSA_LOCAL

Al-Ahsa has its own internal variation. Research on negation documents forms
that differ from Najdi and Hijazi systems. Use only when specifically targeted
and prefer user/native examples if provided.

## 8. NORTHERN_GENERAL

Keep close to Saudi-neutral/Najdi unless the exact city/community is known.

## 9. HAIL_SHAMMARI

High-signal local vocabulary from documented collections:
وشوله، وشبلاك/وشنوحك، وغدان، الوكاد، غاد، مامن، دوك، هقوتي، مشوين، باتسر,
حلولات.

Example:
- الوكاد إن الموضوع أبسط مما تتوقع.
This is strongly local and should not be used in generic Saudi copy.

## 10. SOUTHERN_GENERAL

Because southern variation is large, default to a mild Saudi-neutral register
with southern identity carried by topic/culture rather than invented slang.

## 11. ASIRI_LOCAL

Asir contains Bedouin, Sarawi and Tihami environments with distinct features.
Research documents variation in the definite article, including m- in some
Tihami/Sarawi environments.

Never apply the m- article to all Asiri speech.

## 12. JAZANI_LOCAL

Jazan has multiple local varieties (urban/coastal, village, mountain/tribal,
etc.). Sources document highly local forms such as:
نا = أنا
موطي = نازل
هلبعد / هلبعدا = هذا / هذه in specific communities
ميبشه and related forms in specific Bedouin Jazan varieties

These are intensity-4 material, not generic "southern Saudi".

## 13. NAJRANI_LOCAL / BAHA_LOCAL

Use conservative Saudi-neutral output unless the user supplies native examples
or web verification is available. Avoid borrowing Asiri or Jazani markers just
because all are geographically southern.
